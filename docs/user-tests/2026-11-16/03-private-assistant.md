# The private assistant

From the user test on November 16, 2026. Who took part and how it was run: [README](README.md).

## Chatting with the private assistant

**Completed:** Yes, but the tester found parts of the conversation repetitive.

When the tester entered the chat page, they said there was too much small text. The page felt distracting, and they were unsure where to look.

Their first request was:

> "Chinese restaurant under 70 per person, taste good."

The assistant replied:

> "Got it: Chinese food up to 70 per person and good taste matters to you."

The tester felt that repeating the request was unnecessary. However, they liked how quickly the assistant responded.

The assistant then asked:

> "Do you have any food you cannot eat, such as a diet, religious rule or allergy?"

The tester answered:

> "No."

According to the observer's notes, the assistant responded:

> "Got it. You can't eat anything restricted."

The wording seemed ambiguous. It did not clearly confirm that the tester had no dietary restrictions.

**Moments worth noticing:**

- The small text competed for attention with the main conversation.
- Repeating the request made the interaction feel longer.
- The reply to "no" created uncertainty instead of confirming the answer.

## Second participant's conversation

**Completed:** Yes, although the participant became tired of answering questions.

**Path:** Open invite → Join plan → Enter restaurant preference → Clarify restaurant name → Answer more questions → Finish.

The second participant entered the name of a restaurant they wanted to visit but was unsure whether the assistant understood it.

They also reported being unable to copy and paste in the interface. The exact field and cause were not established.

The participant clarified:

> "The restaurant name is Imperial Treasure."

After that, they felt the assistant had understood.

They also noticed that the assistant repeated each response. They found this redundant, although they said it helped confirm that the assistant had understood them.

As the conversation continued, the participant commented that there were too many questions.

The assistant asked:

> "Is there any kind of food you'd like, and/or any you'd rather not have?"

The participant replied:

> "I like spicy food, but I'm also trying to lose weight."

The observer understood this as wanting something spicy but reasonably healthy, without a strong preference for a particular cuisine.

The assistant's replies included:

> "I don't know that, so I cannot match restaurants to weight goals. Is there any particular kind of food you'd like?"

and:

> "I can't look for spicy food as a kind of food or place, so it won't change which places are picked."

The participant found these responses strange. At this point, they no longer wanted to keep answering and wanted to click "I'm done."

**Moments worth noticing:**

- The participant had to clarify the restaurant name before feeling understood.
- Repeated confirmations added to the length of the conversation.
- The assistant did not turn the more complicated preference into a useful next step.
- The number of questions made the participant want to stop.
